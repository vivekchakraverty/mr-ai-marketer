The app no longer prints the names of the AI models it runs on.

## What changed on screen

Four places named a model. Each kept the half that was worth reading and lost the rest.

| Screen | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Bluesky Post Creator | `hf/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct · 27145 posts watched` | `27,145 posts watched` |
| Mastodon Post Creator | `… · 500 character limit · hf/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct` | `… · 500 character limit` |
| Tumblr Post Creator | a line containing nothing but the model name | gone |
| Marketing Plan | a dropdown of five model identifiers | **Auto (recommended)** and **Alternative engine 1–5** |

The corpus size and the character limit stayed because they tell you something you can act
on. The model names told you what was running under the floor.

## The Marketing Plan still lets you choose

That dropdown is a real choice, not a label — when one engine is busy or refuses a request,
picking another is how you get your plan. So the choice is intact and only the wording
changed; what the app sends when you pick one is exactly what it sent before.

The alternatives are numbered rather than described. Nothing measured here supports calling
one faster or better than another, and an invented ranking would be worse than no ranking.

## Nothing else changed

No behaviour, no generation, no settings. Anything you type in Settings — your own model
repositories, your own buckets — is yours and is untouched.

## Installing

These installers are unsigned. Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will both warn about
that — expected, not a sign anything is wrong. On Windows choose **More info → Run anyway**;
on macOS, right-click the app and choose **Open**.
