# Mr. AI Marketer 0.5.0

A feature release. Tumblr becomes a first-class network — both a place you post from and a
place the app learns from — a new tool joins the Create hub, adding a niche now fills it
immediately instead of leaving it empty for hours, and the Mastodon exemplar pools were
found to be partly built from posts that had failed. The health monitor was rebuilt to
prove the app works without spending inference to do it.

---

## Tumblr Post Creator — new tool

The third post generator, alongside Bluesky and Mastodon. Same idea as both: learn from
posts that actually performed in a niche, then write in that register.

What makes it different is where the corpus comes from. Judging a Tumblr post as
high-engagement needs the author's own baseline, because **Tumblr publishes no follower
count for blogs you do not control** — with or without an API key. Computing that baseline
costs a page of API calls *per blog*, so the crawl lives in a separate collector project
that walks Tumblr's sitemaps at one request per second over days, and the app imports its
output rather than pretending it can do the same work in a background job. The corpus file
is opened read-only, so an import can never disturb a collector that is still running.

**Tumblr brings its own niches.** The first version of the importer matched the corpus
against the niche list the Bluesky and Mastodon tools share, and the result made the
problem obvious: 5,483 of 6,793 posts matched nothing, "ai tools" matched a single post.
Not because the corpus was thin — because those are not the topics Tumblr is organised
around. The collector classifies into 20 niches that genuinely are Tumblr's: `art_design`,
`books_writing`, `lgbtq_community`, `fashion_beauty`, `humor_memes`, `anime_manga`,
`science_space` and so on. Importing on those gives **6,473 posts across 20 niches, every
one with a full pool**.

Those niches are deliberately kept off the shared list, so they never appear in the Bluesky
and Mastodon dropdowns where they would hold nothing. The Tumblr screen therefore *lists*
its niches rather than letting you add them: one appears when the collector classifies
posts into it.

**Honest about its numbers.** Tumblr publishes a single "notes" total — likes, reblogs and
replies summed, never broken down — so that is what is stored, rather than a three-way
split invented to fill the columns. Ranking divides by the blog's own median note count,
which is what the collector uses in place of a follower count, and the app never labels it
"followers". Posts are scored with a prior added to the denominator, measured from the
finished corpus, so one lucky post on a tiny blog cannot dominate a pool.

**It learns from your own posts.** Paste a link into "I published this" and the tool re-reads
that post's note count at 1h, 24h and 48h, then rebuilds the pool. Unlike the Mastodon
loop, this runs when you open the screen rather than on a background timer: Tumblr signs
every read, and your credentials live in the app's encrypted store rather than in the
backend, so a timer would need a copy of them kept somewhere it does not need to be.

Because the corpus was already filtered to viral posts before it arrived, a post of yours
doing perfectly well by your blog's standards would never out-score it — so **your best
qualifying post holds a reserved slot**, shown as "yours" in the draft's exemplar list. It
still has to clear the engagement and prose floors: a guaranteed slot means it does not
have to beat a viral corpus, not that a post nobody engaged with gets shown to the model as
something to imitate.

## Mastodon exemplar pools were partly built from posts that failed

Found by measuring the live corpus, not by reading the code.

**41 of 187 active Mastodon exemplars had zero engagement** — 10 of the 15 in one niche.
The pool took the top N by score with no floor at all, which is fine while a niche has more
measured posts than slots and silently wrong once it has fewer: then everything gets in,
including posts nobody touched. The generator was being shown posts that demonstrably did
not work and told to write like them. A post now needs at least two interactions to
qualify.

**The ranking favoured whoever had fewest followers.** The five highest-rate posts had 5–23
interactions from 52–205 followers, while a post with 158 interactions from 1,924 followers
ranked below all of them; the average follower count of chosen exemplars sat *below* the
corpus average. Ranking now adds a prior to the denominator — every post is scored as
though its author had at least a median-sized following — so a small account has to earn
its place rather than be handed it by division. The stored measurement is unchanged and
still comparable with Bluesky's; only the ordering moved.

**Hashtag walls were being taught as writing.** They clear an engagement floor easily —
tag spam is engagement bait and it works — so a post now needs four words left once
hashtags and links are removed.

Net effect on the live corpus: zero-engagement exemplars **41 → 0**, tag walls **6 → 0**,
average interactions per exemplar **6.9 → 10.0**. Pools shrink where the data is genuinely
thin, which is the honest outcome, and a thin instance already borrows from the wider pool
rather than generating ungrounded.

**A related bug:** the hourly job that measures the posts you publish was writing its
measurements and never rebuilding the pool from them — it called a function with the wrong
number of arguments, and the error was swallowed by a catch-all. So Mastodon recorded how
your posts did and then learned nothing from it. Fixed, with tests.

## Adding a niche now fills it

Creating a niche used to write a row and stop there. Both generators then read an empty
corpus until you found the Collect button or a scheduled job came round — six hours for the
Bluesky ingest, twelve for the Mastodon one. Generation still worked in between, but
silently ungrounded, and the panel showed "0 posts · 0 exemplars" with nothing to say
whether that was a problem or a wait.

A new niche now queues a first fill across both tools at once — a niche is one shared
object, so which screen it was typed on says nothing about where it should be filled from —
and the panel shows progress rather than a misleading zero. Editing an existing niche does
not re-fill it. Measured end to end: from empty to 77 Bluesky posts, 17 from mastodon.social
and 15 from toot.garden, in 35 seconds.

**This uncovered a long-standing bug in the Bluesky cold start.** Its bootstrap ran a 48-hour
backfill across *every* niche at once, capped at 500 posts and unordered. On an established
install, 2,825 posts compete for those slots while a brand-new niche contributes four — so
the new niche's posts never made the cut and its exemplar ranking had nothing to rank.
Every Bluesky niche added on a stocked install has been hitting this. The backfill is now
scoped to the niche being filled; same niche and same data, the result went from
0 exemplars to a working pool.

## Health monitor

Rebuilt around a distinction it did not previously make: **proving the app works, and
paying to generate something, are different jobs.** The daily run now exercises every
module and every capability without generating anything; a separate on-demand mode runs
real generation when you actually want to spend on it.

Endpoints that have nothing to GET — generation, crawling, signed-in fediverse calls — are
held to their request contract instead: the probe sends a body the request model must
reject and requires a rejection back. Nothing is generated, nothing is crawled, no token is
spent, while still proving the router loaded, its request model is intact, and auth let the
call through. That is strictly more than the old "is this path in the schema" check proved,
at about a millisecond each. If such a probe ever *succeeds*, it is reported as degraded
rather than passing — a success means the payload it sends as invalid was accepted, so the
model changed underneath it and the handler just ran for real.

## Engage: Tumblr tab

Your Tumblr dashboard and activity, inside the app. Each network's tab speaks its own
language rather than a lowest common denominator: the Tumblr side reblogs, with or without
a comment, instead of replying — that is what its API can do and how conversation there
actually works — and it treats tags as a real field, because on Tumblr tags are the
distribution.

Tumblr is set up in Settings like every other network, but takes four values rather than
one, because its API is OAuth 1.0a. The README explains where each comes from. The blog
name is optional: leave it blank and the app acts as your primary blog.

## Upgrading

Nothing to migrate. Two things worth doing once after updating:

- **Tumblr Post Creator → Import corpus.** Nothing is imported until you ask for it.
- **Mastodon pools rebuild themselves** on the next collection, so the corrected ranking
  arrives within twelve hours, or immediately if you press Collect.
