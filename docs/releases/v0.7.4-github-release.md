## Fixed: images were silently dropped from distributed posts

A post sent from Distribute with an image attached went out as **text only**. It reported
*Sent*, because it genuinely was — just without the picture.

The engine that posts on your behalf runs in a container and fetches the image from this app
over a small connection the app opens for it. That connection was only being opened when the
app *started* the engine — but the engine is configured to stay running between sessions, so
on almost every launch it was already up, and that path never opened the connection. The
engine then asked for the image, found nothing answering, and posted without it.

Now opened whichever way the engine came up. Confirmed by fetching an image from inside the
running engine container: **HTTP 200, 1.15 MB, image/png**, where before the connection was
refused.

Two things worth knowing about attached images generally:

- The link the engine uses **expires**, by design. An image URL sitting in your send history
  from yesterday will not open today. That is not a fault.
- It is only ever a link to your own machine, valid briefly, and readable only by the engine.

## New: measuring what Tumblr actually rewards

The Tumblr post creator has no "when to post" panel, unlike the other two, and this release
starts the work to find out whether it can have one honestly.

Every timing question asked of the existing Tumblr data fails to reproduce — posting hour
scores +0.06 where the bar is +0.30, and tag count and post length are no better. The reason
is not too little data (there are 6,732 scored posts, more than Mastodon needed) but the
wrong kind: those posts were collected weeks after publication, and a Tumblr note count keeps
climbing through reblogs for weeks. It records how far a post travelled, not anything you
could have decided when you posted it.

So the app now watches a set of active Tumblr blogs and measures each new post again at fixed
ages — one hour, one day, two days. Notes at two days are comparable between posts in a way
lifetime totals never are.

**It runs by itself and it is undemanding.** One request per blog per hour, roughly 15% of
what Tumblr allows, and it does nothing at all unless Tumblr is connected. It survives being
closed: all of its progress lives in the database, so a sweep interrupted halfway continues
from exactly where it stopped rather than starting again.

**It will not invent a measurement.** If the app is closed across a post's one-day mark, that
reading is recorded as missed rather than filled in with a two-day figure. A mislabelled
measurement would quietly poison the very thing this exists to make trustworthy.

**And it may conclude that timing does not matter on Tumblr.** That is a real answer, and if
the data says it, the app will say it too rather than drawing a confident line through noise.
Expect a few weeks before there is enough to judge.

Nothing about this appears on screen yet — it is collection only. The panel, if it is
earned, comes later.

## Installing

These installers are unsigned. Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will both warn about
that — expected, not a sign anything is wrong. On Windows choose **More info → Run anyway**;
on macOS, right-click the app and choose **Open**.
