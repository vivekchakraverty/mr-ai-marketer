Posts can carry video now — and a post that went out can no longer tell you it failed.

## New: add a video to a post

All three post creators — Bluesky, Mastodon and Tumblr — and all three composers on the
Engage screen now take a video from your machine. Pick the file, describe it for people who
cannot see it, and it goes up with the post.

The size limit is shown **before** you open the file dialog, not after a long upload fails,
because the three networks disagree sharply: Bluesky allows about 50MB and roughly three
minutes, Mastodon leaves it to your instance (commonly 40MB, sometimes less), and Tumblr is
the most generous of the three. If a file is too big you are told the number it exceeded
rather than watching an upload run and then break.

## New: embed a YouTube video by pasting its link

A separate box takes a full YouTube URL and puts the video in the post itself. On Tumblr it
becomes a real inline player. On Bluesky and Mastodon it becomes a link card with the
thumbnail and a play affordance, which is as close to an embed as those two go — neither
protocol has an inline player for someone else's video.

Nothing is asked of YouTube beyond the public information attached to the link.

## Fixed: Mastodon refused every video

Attaching a video to a Mastodon post failed with:

> Cannot attach files that have not finished processing. Try again in a moment!

Mastodon accepts a video and then **converts it in the background**, and it cannot be
attached to a post until that finishes. The app was treating the upload as done the moment
it was accepted — which is correct for images, and only for images, which is why this had
never come up.

The app now waits for the conversion, up to a minute. If a server is still working after
that you are told it is likely too long or too large, rather than handed the raw error.

Confirmed by posting a real clip: **1280×720, 10 seconds, with its description intact.**

## Fixed: videos posted to Bluesky arrived without a shape

A video posted to Bluesky carried no dimensions, so apps reading your feed reserved a
default-sized box and the timeline **jumped** when the video loaded and turned out to be a
different shape.

The app now measures the file first. Specifically, it reads how the video is meant to be
*displayed*, not merely how it is stored — a phone records a portrait video as a sideways
one plus a note saying "turn this a quarter turn", and using the stored dimensions would put
a portrait clip in a landscape box. Getting that wrong is worse than leaving it out, so
anything the app cannot measure confidently is posted without dimensions, exactly as before.

Confirmed on a real post, and on a deliberately rotated file to check the portrait case.

## Fixed: a post that was sent could report itself as failed

This one is not about video and affects **every** post sent to Bluesky.

Bluesky stores your post in one place and indexes it for reading in another, and the index
lags a moment behind. The app was writing the post and then immediately looking for it in
the index — and when the index had not caught up, reporting:

> Post was not found on Bluesky.

for a post that had gone out perfectly. It is a race, so it happened only sometimes, which
is the worst version of this problem: the natural response to that message is to send the
post again, and end up with two.

The app now gives the index a few seconds, and if it is still behind, shows you the post it
just sent rather than claiming a failure. **A post that was created can no longer be
reported as an error.**

## What has and has not been posted for real

Worth stating plainly, because "it compiles" and "it posts" are different claims:

- **Bluesky video** and **Mastodon video** were each confirmed by posting a real clip to a
  real account and checking it played. Both test posts were deleted afterwards.
- **Tumblr video** has not been posted live — that account's credentials live in the app
  rather than on a test bench. The path is built the same way as the other two and is
  covered by tests, but it has not carried a real file yet.
- **YouTube embeds** are covered by tests but have not been posted live on every network.

## Installing

These installers are unsigned. Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will both warn about
that — expected, not a sign anything is wrong. On Windows choose **More info → Run anyway**;
on macOS, right-click the app and choose **Open**.
