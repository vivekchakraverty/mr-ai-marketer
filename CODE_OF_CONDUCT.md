# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they're trying their best with less context than you have.

## What that means here

This project is maintained by one person in their own time, and most people who show up will
be users rather than developers. So:

- **Criticise the code, not the person.** "This breaks when the token is empty" is useful.
  "Whoever wrote this clearly didn't think" is not, and it isn't more true for being harsher.
- **Assume good faith.** A vague bug report usually means someone doesn't know what detail
  helps, not that they're being lazy. Ask for what you need.
- **No harassment**, in issues, pull requests, commit messages, or anywhere else this project
  is being discussed. That covers personal attacks, demeaning comments about anyone's
  background or identity, sexual attention or imagery, deliberate misgendering, and publishing
  anyone's private information.
- **Don't derail.** Issues are for the thing they're about.
- **Take "no" without a fight.** Not every suggestion fits the project, and a maintainer
  declining one isn't an insult. You're welcome to fork.

Nobody owes you a response, a fix, or a merged pull request. Equally, nobody here has to put
up with being treated badly in exchange for free software.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour is a problem, email **vivekchakraverty@gmail.com**. Include links if
you have them. Reports are handled privately, and the person who reports won't be named to the
person reported without their agreement.

## What happens then

Depending on what happened and whether it's a pattern: a private word, a public correction, a
deleted comment, or a block from the repository. The maintainer decides, and doesn't have to
justify a block to the person blocked.

## Scope

Anywhere this project is the subject: the repository, its issues and pull requests and
discussions, and any space where someone is representing the project.

---

If you'd rather work from a fuller, more formal standard — most projects do, and it covers
edge cases this doesn't — the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org)
is the usual reference, and nothing above is meant to contradict it.
